# Unified AISTATS v2 study protocol

**Status:** theory and experiment scope locked around one question. This document supersedes broader event-mechanism, phase-family, memory, gate, and semiparametric directions.

## 1. Scientific question

The paper asks:

> A bounded state is observed only through its net change, but that change may be generated by a nonnegative damage/interruption flow and a nonnegative restoration flow. When is one signed flow sufficient, and when is it worth estimating the two opposing flows separately?

Power outages are the application. Event transfer is an evaluation setting and a theoretical corollary, not the paper's defining question.

## 2. Model classes

The two-flow conditional transition is

\[
m_x(y)=D(x)(1-y)-R(x)y,
\qquad D(x),R(x)\ge0.
\]

`D` is called `U` in the code. The proposed neural model parameterizes `D` and `R` separately.

The theory-matched one-flow class is

\[
\mathcal F_1(x)
=\{a(1-y):a\ge0\}
\cup
\{-b y:b\ge0\}.
\]

Its neural implementation is `net_scaled`: one signed rate may switch direction across driver values, but at a fixed driver value it cannot maintain positive damage and restoration simultaneously.

## 3. Core theory

For environment `e`, define

\[
A_e(x)=\mathbb E_e[(1-Y)^2\mid X=x],
\quad
B_e(x)=\mathbb E_e[Y^2\mid X=x],
\quad
v_e(x)=\operatorname{Var}_e(Y\mid X=x).
\]

### Theorem 1: when two flows are representationally necessary

The exact oracle error of the best one-flow approximation is

\[
\boxed{
G_e(x)
=
v_e(x)
\min\left\{
\frac{R(x)^2}{A_e(x)},
\frac{D(x)^2}{B_e(x)}
\right\}.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
G_e(x)>0
\iff
v_e(x)>0,
\quad D(x)>0,
\quad R(x)>0.
\]

This is the primary theorem. It directly distinguishes the regimes:

- only one physical flow active: one flow is correctly specified;
- no conditional state variation: one flow is sufficient for prediction and two flows are not separately identifiable;
- both flows active with state variation: one flow has an irreducible approximation gap.

### Theorem 2: when the two-flow representation is worth its estimation cost

In a local fixed-design Gaussian benchmark with `n` observations and noise variance `sigma^2`, the two-flow least-squares fitted mean has expected error

\[
2\sigma^2/n,
\]

whereas an oracle-selected one-flow fitted mean has expected error

\[
G_n+\sigma^2/n.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{two flow is favored}
\iff
G_n>\sigma^2/n.
}
\]

The dimensionless selection index is

\[
\Gamma_n=nG_n/\sigma^2.
\]

`Gamma_n<1` is the parsimonious one-flow regime; `Gamma_n>1` is the two-flow regime. For neural functions this remains a benchmark: effective complexity replaces the literal one-parameter difference.

### Supporting identification result

With

\[
Q(x)=\mathbb E[\phi(Y)\phi(Y)^\top\mid X=x],
\qquad
\phi(Y)=(1-Y,-Y)^\top,
\]

\[
\det Q(x)=v(x),
\qquad
v(x)\le\lambda_{\min}(Q(x))\le2v(x).
\]

This result answers whether `D(x)` and `R(x)` can be learned separately. It is a supporting condition, not an independent performance mechanism.

### Event shift as a corollary

If a one-flow projection is fitted under source event `P` and evaluated under target event `Q`, an additional nonnegative projection-shift term appears. This can move a problem toward the two-flow regime, but no theorem says event holdout must always increase the empirical two-flow gain.

The complete statements and proofs are in `docs/CORE_THEORY_V2.md`.

## 6. Proposed neural model

The architecture remains unchanged:

\[
Y_{t+1}
=
Y_t+D_\theta(X_t)(1-Y_t)-R_\theta(X_t)Y_t.
\]

`D_theta` and `R_theta` are separate width-32, two-layer, nonnegative bounded neural networks. No semiparametric component, event embedding, recurrent state, recovery burden, distributed lag, damage gate, transformer, or second damage head is included.

The structural comparator `net_scaled` is parameter matched within one percent and differs only in replacing the two concurrent nonnegative flows by one signed flow.

## 7. Training objective

For source event `e`, define the masked 24-hour rollout loss

\[
L_{\mathrm{roll},e}
=
\frac{\sum_{i,t\le24}M_{eit}(\widehat Y_{eit}-Y_{eit})^2}
{\sum_{i,t\le24}M_{eit}},
\]

and teacher-forced one-step transition loss

\[
L_{\mathrm{step},e}
=
\frac{\sum_{i,t\le24}\widetilde M_{eit}
(\widehat Y^{\mathrm{TF}}_{ei,t+1}-Y_{ei,t+1})^2}
{\sum_{i,t\le24}\widetilde M_{eit}}.
\]

The fixed event-balanced objective is

\[
\boxed{
L_{\mathrm{train}}
=
\frac1{|\mathcal E_{\mathrm{train}}|}
\sum_e
\left[
\frac12L_{\mathrm{roll},e}
+
\frac12L_{\mathrm{step},e}
\right].
}
\]

The one-step term aligns estimation with the conditional drift studied by the theory; it is not a separate scientific contribution. Its coefficient is fixed and not swept.

## 8. Validation and optimization

Each outer fold holds out one complete test event. Validation events are selected without using outage outcomes or model results, using the fixed map in `configs/event_split_map_g2_two_validation.json`; all remaining events train the model.

The validation score is the equal-event mean of

\[
\frac12L_{\mathrm{path24},e}
+
\frac14L_{6,e}
+
\frac14L_{24,e}.
\]

All neural arms share optimizer, budget, masks, rows, preprocessing, initialization calibration, and checkpoint rule.

## 9. One main real-data experiment

Run eleven leave-one-event-out folds and three neural seeds. Compare exactly:

1. `two_rate_v2` — proposed two-flow NN;
2. `net_scaled_v2` — parameter-matched one-flow NN;
3. `hgb_same_information` — strong direct-regression baseline;
4. `damped_persistence` — simple operational baseline.

The main table reports equal-event RMSE for readability and event-level MSE differences for inference at h+6 and h+24.

The primary scientific contrast is

\[
R_{\mathrm{one},e,h}-R_{\mathrm{two},e,h}.
\]

HGB and persistence answer whether the model is practically competitive; they do not test the one-flow/two-flow theorem.

## 10. Ablation structure

The paper has one central structural ablation and one controlled theory ablation.

### A1. Collapse two flows to one

`two_rate_v2` versus `net_scaled_v2` is the direct empirical test of the model-selection question. It removes simultaneous damage/restoration flow decomposition while retaining comparable neural capacity and state scaling.

### A2. Controlled flow-selection phase diagram

A single synthetic experiment varies only

\[
D,\quad R,\quad v,\quad n,\quad\sigma^2.
\]

It verifies:

- `G=0` when `D=0`, `R=0`, or `v=0`;
- the exact oracle-gap formula when both flows are active;
- the one-flow/two-flow risk crossover at `Gamma_n=1`;
- rate recovery deterioration as `lambda_min(Q)` approaches zero.

This is one coherent ablation, not several unrelated synthetic campaigns.

Removal of the one-step loss may be reported only as an appendix optimization check. It is not a headline ablation.

## 11. Statistical reporting

For each event and horizon, average neural seeds first. Use the event as the independent unit. Report:

- all eleven event-level MSE differences;
- equal-event mean and median;
- event bootstrap interval;
- exact paired sign-flip/randomization test;
- positive-event count;
- leave-one-event influence.

Do not treat fold-by-seed rows as independent observations.

## 12. Contribution hierarchy

The paper presents:

1. **When one flow is sufficient and when two are necessary:** the exact oracle one-flow gap.
2. **When the extra flow is statistically worth estimating:** the fixed-design representation-estimation threshold and flow-selection index.
3. **A theory-aligned neural evaluation:** one event-held-out performance experiment, with one-flow collapse as the central ablation and HGB/persistence as external baselines.

Identification geometry supports the second point. Event projection shift extends the first point to distribution shift. Neither replaces the central one-flow-versus-two-flow question.
