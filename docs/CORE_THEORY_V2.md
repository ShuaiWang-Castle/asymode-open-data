# Core theory v3: when one flow is enough and when two opposing flows are worth estimating

This note fixes the theoretical center of the AISTATS paper.

The paper is **not primarily about event shift**. It is about a model-selection question:

> An observed bounded state is the net result of a nonnegative damage/interruption flow and a nonnegative restoration flow. When is one signed flow sufficient, and when is it worth estimating the two flows separately?

Event shift is a corollary: it can make the one-flow approximation more fragile, but it is not the defining contribution.

## 1. Two model classes

At a fixed driver value `x`, write the true conditional drift as

\[
m_x(y)=D(x)(1-y)-R(x)y,
\qquad D(x),R(x)\ge 0,
\]

where `D` is the damage/interruption flow and `R` is the restoration flow. (`D` is called `U` in the current code.)

The **two-flow class** is

\[
\mathcal F_2(x)
=\{d(1-y)-r y:d,r\ge0\}.
\]

The parameter-matched **one-flow class** is

\[
\mathcal F_1(x)
=\{a(1-y):a\ge0\}
\cup
\{-b y:b\ge0\}.
\]

Thus one signed function may predict damage at some driver values and restoration at others, but at a fixed `x` it must choose one direction. It cannot represent simultaneous positive damage and restoration flows. This is the population analogue of `net_scaled`.

For a target environment `e`, define

\[
A_e(x)=\mathbb E_e[(1-Y)^2\mid X=x],
\quad
B_e(x)=\mathbb E_e[Y^2\mid X=x],
\quad
C_e(x)=\mathbb E_e[Y(1-Y)\mid X=x],
\]

and

\[
v_e(x)=\operatorname{Var}_e(Y\mid X=x)
       =A_e(x)B_e(x)-C_e(x)^2.
\]

## 2. Headline theorem: exact cost of using one flow

### Theorem 1 (oracle one-flow approximation gap)

At fixed `x`, the best one-flow approximation to the two-flow drift satisfies

\[
\boxed{
G_e(x)
:=
\inf_{g\in\mathcal F_1(x)}
\mathbb E_e[(m_x(Y)-g(Y))^2\mid X=x]
=
v_e(x)
\min\left\{
\frac{R(x)^2}{A_e(x)},
\frac{D(x)^2}{B_e(x)}
\right\}.
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
G_e(x)=0
\iff
v_e(x)=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
D(x)=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
R(x)=0.
}
\]

Equivalently, a two-flow representation has a strict population advantage exactly when

\[
\boxed{
v_e(x)>0,
\qquad D(x)>0,
\qquad R(x)>0.
}
\]

This theorem gives the direct answer to “one flow or two?” at the representation level:

- **One flow is sufficient** when only damage is active, only restoration is active, or the same driver value is observed at only one state so the two flows are observationally indistinguishable.
- **Two flows are necessary** when damage and restoration are both active and the state varies at the same driver value.

### Proof

For the damage-only branch, minimize

\[
L_D(a)=\mathbb E_e[((D-a)(1-Y)-RY)^2\mid X=x].
\]

The unconstrained minimizer is

\[
a_e^*=D-R\frac{C_e}{A_e},
\]

and, when it is nonnegative,

\[
L_D(a_e^*)=R^2\frac{A_eB_e-C_e^2}{A_e}
          =R^2\frac{v_e}{A_e}.
\]

For the restoration-only branch,

\[
b_e^*=R-D\frac{C_e}{B_e},
\qquad
L_R(b_e^*)=D^2\frac{v_e}{B_e}.
\]

At least one of the two unconstrained branch minimizers is nonnegative whenever `v_e>0`. Moreover, if the damage-branch minimizer is negative, then

\[
\frac{D}{R}<\frac{C_e}{A_e}\le\sqrt{\frac{B_e}{A_e}},
\]

so

\[
D^2/B_e<R^2/A_e,
\]

and the restoration branch is the minimizing feasible branch. The symmetric argument applies if the restoration-branch minimizer is negative. Taking the smaller branch risk yields the stated formula, including the nonnegative constraints.

## 3. Headline finite-sample result: representation versus estimation

Theorem 1 says when two flows are needed in the population. It does **not** say that a fitted two-flow model always predicts better, because two flows cost an additional degree of freedom.

Consider a local fixed-design Gaussian benchmark with `n` observations:

\[
Z_i=D(1-y_i)-R y_i+\varepsilon_i,
\qquad
\varepsilon_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2).
\]

Let `m=(m_x(y_1),...,m_x(y_n))^T`. Let `P_2` be the orthogonal projector onto the two-column design span generated by `(1-y_i)` and `(-y_i)`. Let `P_1^*` be the better of the two rank-one projectors corresponding to the damage-only and restoration-only branches. The branch is chosen by an oracle, so this is a favorable benchmark for one flow.

Define the empirical oracle collapse gap

\[
G_n
=
\frac1n\|(I-P_1^*)m\|_2^2.
\]

### Theorem 2 (fixed-design flow-selection threshold)

If the two-flow design has rank two, then

\[
\mathbb E\left[
\frac1n\|P_2Z-m\|_2^2
\middle|y_1,\ldots,y_n
\right]
=
\frac{2\sigma^2}{n},
\]

whereas the oracle one-flow estimator satisfies

\[
\mathbb E\left[
\frac1n\|P_1^*Z-m\|_2^2
\middle|y_1,\ldots,y_n
\right]
=
G_n+rac{\sigma^2}{n}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{two-flow has lower expected fitted-mean error}
\iff
G_n>\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
}
\]

Equivalently, define the dimensionless flow-selection index

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_n:=\frac{nG_n}{\sigma^2}.
}
\]

Then

\[
\Gamma_n<1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{one flow is favored by parsimony},
\]

\[
\Gamma_n>1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{the representation gain of two flows exceeds its extra variance cost}.
\]

### Proof

Because `m` lies in the two-column design span,

\[
P_2Z-m=P_2\varepsilon,
\]

so

\[
\mathbb E\|P_2\varepsilon\|_2^2
=\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(P_2)=2\sigma^2.
\]

For the oracle one-flow projector,

\[
P_1^*Z-m
=-(I-P_1^*)m+P_1^*\varepsilon.
\]

The deterministic bias term is orthogonal to the projected noise term, hence

\[
\mathbb E\|P_1^*Z-m\|_2^2
=\|(I-P_1^*)m\|_2^2
+\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(P_1^*)
=nG_n+\sigma^2.
\]

Divide by `n` and compare.

### Interpretation beyond the local linear benchmark

For neural conditional functions, the exact extra estimation cost is not literally `sigma^2/n`; it depends on effective complexity, regularization, shared information across nearby `x`, and misspecification. The robust qualitative criterion is

\[
\boxed{
\text{use two flows when}
\quad
\text{oracle collapse gap}
>
\text{extra estimation cost of the second flow}.
}
\]

The fixed-design theorem supplies an exact benchmark rather than claiming that a neural-network risk difference equals `sigma^2/n`.

## 4. Supporting theorem: can the two flows be learned separately?

Let

\[
\phi(Y)=(1-Y,-Y)^\top,
\qquad
Q_e(x)=\mathbb E_e[\phi(Y)\phi(Y)^\top\mid X=x].
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\det Q_e(x)=v_e(x),
\qquad
v_e(x)\le\lambda_{\min}(Q_e(x))\le2v_e(x).
}
\]

Hence `D(x)` and `R(x)` are locally identifiable exactly when `v_e(x)>0`.

This is not the headline theorem. Its role is to distinguish **necessity** from **learnability**:

- `G_e(x)` measures how much prediction is lost by collapsing to one flow.
- `lambda_min(Q_e(x))` measures how stably the two flows can be separated from data.
- `n`, noise, and model complexity determine whether the representation benefit survives estimation.

The resulting phase diagram is:

| Regime | Population structure | Statistical conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| `D=0` or `R=0` | only one physical flow is active | one flow is correctly specified |
| `v=0` | two flows cannot be distinguished at this `x` | one flow is sufficient for prediction and two flows are not identifiable |
| `G>0` but `Gamma<1` | two flows exist, but signal is weak relative to sample/noise | one flow can predict better |
| `G>0` and `Gamma>1` | both flows matter and are estimable | two-flow model is favored |

This is the conceptual center of the paper.

## 5. Event shift is a corollary, not the core

Suppose a damage-only one-flow projection is fitted under source-event distribution `P`:

\[
a_P^*=D-R\frac{C_P}{A_P}.
\]

When evaluated under target event `Q`, its risk is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_Q^+(a_P^*)
=
R^2\frac{v_Q}{A_Q}
+
R^2A_Q
\left(
\frac{C_P}{A_P}-\frac{C_Q}{A_Q}
\right)^2.
}
\]

The first term is the target oracle one-flow gap from Theorem 1. The second is an additional source-to-target projection-shift penalty. The restoration-only expression is symmetric.

Thus event shift can increase the effective cost of using one flow, but it does not change the fundamental question. The fundamental question remains whether the one-flow approximation error, including any shift penalty, exceeds the extra estimation cost of two flows.

No theorem states that event holdout must empirically increase the two-flow advantage on every dataset or horizon.

## 6. Exact mapping to experiments

The paper has one main real-data experiment and one controlled theoretical ablation.

### Main real-data experiment

Use all eleven `g2-convective-11` events under leave-one-complete-event-out evaluation. Compare:

1. neural two-flow dynamics;
2. parameter-matched neural one-flow `net_scaled`;
3. HGB on the same information;
4. damped persistence.

The primary scientific comparison is **two flow versus one flow**. HGB and persistence establish forecasting competitiveness but do not test the theorem.

The main table reports h+6 and h+24 performance. Event-level differences, not fold-by-seed pseudo-replications, are the inferential units.

### Structural ablation in the main table

`two_rate` versus `net_scaled` removes only simultaneous damage/restoration decomposition. This is the direct empirical test of Theorems 1–2.

### Controlled flow-regime ablation

A single synthetic phase diagram varies only:

\[
D,\quad R,\quad v,\quad n,\quad \sigma^2.
\]

It must show:

1. zero oracle gap when `D=0`, `R=0`, or `v=0`;
2. the exact formula for `G` when both flows are active;
3. the crossover at `Gamma_n=1` between oracle one-flow and two-flow fitted-mean risk;
4. deterioration of rate recovery as `lambda_min(Q)` approaches zero.

This is one unified ablation, not several unrelated synthetic experiments.

### Objective ablation

Removal of the teacher-forced one-step term is optional appendix material. It is an optimization check, not a theoretical contribution, and it must not compete with the one-flow/two-flow story for space.

## 7. Claims explicitly excluded

The paper does not claim:

- two flows always predict better than one flow;
- event holdout necessarily enlarges the two-flow advantage;
- local identifiability alone predicts empirical performance;
- learned damage/restoration functions are causal hazards;
- event-family phase ordering is explained by conditional state variance;
- a second observed outage wave requires a second damage network;
- the model dominates HGB at every forecast horizon.
