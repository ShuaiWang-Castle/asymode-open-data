# Related-work pre-check: what the closest three papers actually do

Read in full, not from abstracts. The question was narrow: do they carry formal
theory, how are they organised, and what do they run?

## Summary

| | Chen et al., arXiv 2502.18321 | Ziarelli et al., arXiv 2410.11545 | Zhu et al., arXiv 2109.09711 |
|---|---|---|---|
| framing | IEEE power systems | scientific ML / applied math | INFORMS operations research |
| theorems / propositions / lemmas | **none** | **none** | **none** |
| theory-flavoured analysis | none | phase-line and equilibrium stability of the rate ODE | none |
| identifiability | not discussed | discussed **informally**, in words | not discussed |
| synthetic experiment | yes — generated by SIR | yes — rate law prescribed a priori, then recovered | no |
| held-out evaluation | train/test on synthetic + real | test set, noise levels, hyperparameter sweep | **in-sample estimation** |
| baselines | two-stage predict-then-optimize | none (self-comparisons only) | Hawkes with constant intensity; naive persistence |
| headline metric | decision regret | generalization error on test set | MSE |

**No formal result appears in any of the three.** A single exactly-stated
identifiability condition, proved and then verified numerically, would therefore
be a real differentiator rather than table stakes.

## Chen, Fioretto, Qiu, Zhu — Global-Decision-Focused Neural ODEs

Structure: I Introduction (I.A Related Work) · II Predict-All-Then-Optimize-Globally
· III Global-Decision-Focused Neural ODEs · IV Application: mobile generator
deployment · V Experiments · VI Conclusion · Appendix (9 subsections).

Contributions claimed: a paradigm (PATOG), a method (GDF Neural ODEs),
experiments on synthetic data, and evaluation on a customer-level outage dataset.

Experiments: real data is the 2018 Nor'easter in Massachusetts — county-level
customer outage counts, HRRR meteorology, ACS socioeconomics. Two decision tasks:
mobile generator deployment and line undergrounding. Baselines are two-stage
predict-then-optimize variants. Metric is **decision regret**, reported as up to
75% reduction.

**The synthetic data is generated by a simplified SIR model, and the model being
evaluated is in the SIR family.** The generator and the hypothesis share their
functional form. Our synthetic study uses a served-pool exponent of 1.5, which
none of the compared arms implements, precisely so the comparison cannot be
dismissed on this ground. Worth stating plainly and without disparagement — it is
a design difference a reviewer will notice either way.

**Their choice of decision regret over RMSE is directly relevant to our open
metric question.** There is published precedent in this exact application area for
not making RMSE the headline.

## Ziarelli, Pagani, Parolini, Regazzoni, Verani — learning transmission-rate dynamics

**This is the paper whose shape ours should follow.** Not the power-systems ones.

Structure: 1 Introduction · 2 Methods · 3 Results (3.1 synthetic test case, with
3.1.1 Data generation and 3.1.2 Hyperparameter setup; 3.2 real influenza case,
with 3.2.1 / 3.2.2 / 3.2.3) · 4 Conclusions · Appendix A: derivation of the
synthetic model.

Architecture is hybrid: a data-driven layer learns the transmission rate's
dynamics from exogenous variables, integrated numerically, and the resulting rate
series is fed into a physics-based SEIR layer.

Experiments: (a) a synthetic case where **the rate's differential law is
prescribed a priori and then recovered**, with several noise levels and
uncertainty bands taken as 0.1–0.9 quantiles over 20 runs; (b) a sensitivity
campaign over network topology, observation-window width and training-set size;
(c) real data — four influenza epidemics in Italy, 2010–2020, with temperature and
relative humidity as exogenous drivers; (d) a data-assimilation step adapting the
learned equation to a specific wave.

Two things to take from it. Their synthetic recovery experiment is structurally
what ours is, which is reassurance that the design is publishable. And they reach
the identifiability point but only in words: they observe that from a low initial
rate the wave never breaks out, the trajectory becomes independent of the rate
dynamics, and a parameter is then hardly identifiable. **That is the same
phenomenon our determinant argument states exactly.** Stating it as a condition
with a proof, rather than as an observation about one simulation, is available and
unclaimed.

They do carry genuine dynamical-systems analysis — a phase line for the rate
equation with two equilibria whose stability flips on the sign of a temperature
threshold — but it is presented as derivation, not as numbered results.

## Zhu, Yao, Xie, Qiu, Qiu, Wu — Quantifying Grid Resilience

Structure: 1 Introduction (1.1 Related work) · 2 Motivating example · 3 Grid
resilience modeling (3.1 Accumulation of weather effect, 3.2 Spatio-temporal
non-homogeneous Poisson process) · 4 Numerical results (4.1 Predictive
performance, 4.2 Descriptive analysis, 4.3 Resilience quantification, 4.4
Resilience enhancement analysis) · 5 Discussion · Appendix A (34 weather
variables).

Model: weather effects enter as a discounted accumulation with a learnable decay
rate per variable, feeding a non-homogeneous Poisson intensity through a
128-128-1 network. Fitted on Massachusetts service-territory data. Baselines are
a Hawkes process with constant base intensity, and naive persistence. Metric is
MSE, and the predictive evaluation is **in-sample**.

**This paper is the foil.** It states as a premise that outage occurrence and
restoration happen concurrently and therefore that "separately modeling these two
dynamics is not practical" (Zhu et al., §3.2), and models the net process
directly for that reason. Our entire contribution is that the separation is both
practical and necessary, and we have the onset evidence to say why. An
introduction can be built against that sentence.

## What this changes for us

1. **A stated and proved identifiability condition is worth carrying.** None of
   the three has one; the closest gets there in prose. Ours is already [A].
2. **Follow the applied-maths structure, not the power-systems one.** Methods,
   then synthetic recovery, then real data, then sensitivity.
3. **Our baseline suite would be unusually strong for this literature**, where the
   norm is persistence plus one alternative. County-held-out folds across storms
   appear in none of them; one paper evaluates in-sample.
4. **RMSE as headline is not obligatory here.** There is precedent for a
   decision-relevant metric, which is one way out of the problem that an all-zero
   predictor scores well on this target.
