Identifiability and the Value of Separating Interruption from Restoration}
\aistatsauthor{Anonymous Author(s)}
\aistatsaddress{Anonymous Institution}
]

\begin{abstract}
An observed outage trajectory records only a net change, although customer interruptions and restorations may occur concurrently. We study when one signed, state-scaled flow is sufficient and when the two conditional-mean components should be represented separately. For the one-step mean $m_0(x,y)=U_0(x)(1-y)-R_0(x)y$, the exact loss from suppressing one component factors as $G(x)=v(x)\kappa(x)$, where $v(x)$ is conditional state variance and $\kappa(x)$ measures coactivity. In a local Gaussian benchmark, the selection index $\Gamma_n=(n v/\sigma_\varepsilon^2)\kappa$ is turnover information times coactivity. An exactly solvable two-state case exhibits the predicted crossover and shows why identifiability is necessary but not sufficient for predictive improvement. We evaluate parameter-matched neural classes on eleven leave-one-storm-event-out folds of public county outage and reanalysis-weather data. Relative to the signed one-flow network, the two-flow model lowers equal-event RMSE by $4.55\%$ at 24 hours, but the pre-registered confirmatory gate is not met; the 48-hour interval includes zero. Gradient boosting and damped persistence remain stronger in mean long-horizon RMSE. The result supports a model-selection interpretation rather than universal dominance: separation removes a calculable representation restriction, while estimation, pooling validity, and rollout propagation determine whether it matters in practice.
\end{abstract}
