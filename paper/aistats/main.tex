Identifiability and the Value of Separating Interruption from Restoration}
\aistatsauthor{Anonymous Authors}
\aistatsaddress{Anonymous Institution}
]

\begin{abstract}
County outage trajectories record only net change even when interruptions and restorations occur concurrently. We ask when one signed, state-scaled flow is sufficient and when the two conditional-mean components should be represented separately. For $m_0=U_0(1-y)-R_0y$, the exact loss from suppressing one component is $G=v\kappa$, where $v$ is conditional state variance and $\kappa$ measures coactivity. In a local Gaussian benchmark, $\Gamma_n=nG_n/\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ factors into turnover information and coactivity; two flows have lower expected fitted-mean error when $\Gamma_n>1$. An exactly solvable two-state case exhibits this crossover and clarifies why identifiability is necessary but not sufficient for prediction. We evaluate parameter-matched neural classes on eleven leave-one-storm-event-out folds of public county outage and reanalysis-weather data. Relative to the signed one-flow network, the two-flow model lowers equal-event RMSE by $4.55\%$ at 24 hours, but the pre-registered confirmatory gate is not met; the 48-hour interval includes zero. Gradient boosting and damped persistence remain stronger in mean long-horizon RMSE. Separation therefore removes a calculable representation restriction, while estimation, pooling validity, and rollout propagation determine whether the restriction matters in practice.
\end{abstract}
