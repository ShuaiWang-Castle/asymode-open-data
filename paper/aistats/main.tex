\begin{abstract}
An observed trajectory records only a net change, although that change may be generated by two simultaneous nonnegative processes.  We study when a single signed flow is sufficient and when damage/interruption and restoration should be modeled separately.  For the conditional drift
\[
 m_0(x,y)=D_0(x)(1-y)-R_0(x)y,
\]
we derive the exact population loss of the best state-scaled one-flow approximation.  If
$A(x)=\E[(1-Y)^2\mid X=x]$, $B(x)=\E[Y^2\mid X=x]$, and
$v(x)=\Var(Y\mid X=x)$, then the oracle collapse gap is
\[
 G(x)=v(x)\min\!\left\{\frac{R_0(x)^2}{A(x)},
                         \frac{D_0(x)^2}{B(x)}\right\}.
\]
Thus one flow is sufficient exactly when one physical flow is absent or the conditional state is degenerate; otherwise the two-flow class has a strict representation advantage.  We then separate representation from estimation.  In an explicitly relaxed local Gaussian benchmark, the extra rank of the two-flow fit is repaid when $\Gamma_n=nG_n/\sigma^2>1$, and the realized risk comparison has an exact $\chi^2_1$ distribution.  A supporting identification result shows that the conditional Gram determinant equals $v(x)$ and that its smallest eigenvalue lies between $v(x)$ and $2v(x)$.  Hence the same state dispersion that makes two flows observationally distinct also controls how stably they can be learned.  Distribution shift enters only as an extension: a one-flow projection learned in one event acquires an additional nonnegative projection-shift penalty in another.  We instantiate the model with two small neural rate networks and specify one leave-one-event-out study on public county-level power-outage data, with a parameter-matched one-flow network as the theorem-matched comparator.  Final empirical cells are populated only from the locked experiment.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:introduction}

A scalar state may rise and fall because two processes act at the same time.  During a severe-weather event, some customers lose service while other customers are restored; in a compartment model, units enter and leave a state; in an inventory system, replenishment and demand operate concurrently.  Forecasting models commonly replace these opposing processes by a single signed update.  This simplification reduces variance and computation, but it also changes the statistical object being learned: a signed net flow need not equal either underlying process.

Power-outage evolution makes this distinction concrete.  Utility resilience curves can be decomposed retrospectively into overlapping outage and restoration processes \citep{carrington2021}.  Public county-level records, however, expose only the aggregate fraction of customers without power.  The statistical question is therefore not whether two processes can be named after the fact, but whether predicting them separately has value when only their net effect is observed.

We ask one model-selection question:
\begin{quote}
\emph{When is one signed flow sufficient for prediction, and when does separating damage and restoration provide enough representation benefit to justify estimating an additional function?}
\end{quote}

The question has three layers.  First, two flows may be \emph{unnecessary}: if only damage is active, only restoration is active, or the same driver value is observed at a single state, one signed flow can reproduce the conditional mean exactly.  Second, two flows may be necessary but difficult to estimate.  Their separation is weak when the conditional state distribution is concentrated.  Third, even identifiable two-flow structure may fail to improve finite-sample prediction because the second function adds estimation error.  The paper develops these layers in that order.

Our contributions are tightly coupled.

\paragraph{Exact one-flow approximation gap.}
We characterize the population cost of collapsing two nonnegative flows into one signed, state-scaled flow.  The gap is zero if and only if damage is absent, restoration is absent, or the conditional state is degenerate.  This gives a direct, nonasymptotic answer at the representation level.

\paragraph{Representation--estimation phase diagram.}
We derive the information geometry of the two-flow regression and give an exact local Gaussian benchmark for the additional degree of freedom.  The benchmark is deliberately labeled as a relaxed linear-span result: it is not asserted to be the exact generalization risk of a constrained neural network.  Its role is to isolate the bias--variance crossover that the real-data comparison must resolve.

\paragraph{One theory-aligned neural study.}
We retain a deliberately small two-flow neural state equation and compare it with a parameter-matched one-flow network, histogram gradient boosting on the same information, and damped persistence.  The primary scientific contrast is two flows versus one; the external baselines establish forecasting context.  A single controlled phase-diagram experiment varies only the quantities in the theory.

The current contribution is not a claim that mechanistic structure always beats a flexible predictor.  Rather, it identifies the structural benefit of two flows and states explicitly the additional estimation and misspecification costs that can overturn that benefit.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Varying-coefficient regression and identifiability.}
At a fixed driver value, our transition equation is a two-column varying-coefficient regression, a classical statistical construction \citep{hastie1993}.  The novelty claimed here is not the existence of varying-coefficient models.  It is the exact geometry induced by the particular basis $(1-y,-y)$ and the resulting one-flow-versus-two-flow model-selection criterion.  Neural ordinary differential equations and neural differential models provide flexible parameterizations of unknown dynamics \citep{chen2018}; our analysis instead focuses on when two opposing conditional components are observationally distinguishable and predictively worth estimating.

\paragraph{Outage and restoration dynamics.}
Resilience curves generated from utility records can be decomposed into outage and restoration processes that overlap in time \citep{carrington2021}.  The EAGLE-I data provide county-level outage estimates at 15-minute resolution and document important coverage limitations \citep{brelsford2024,tansakul2023}.  We use ERA5 hourly reanalysis as the exogenous weather source \citep{hersbach2020}.  Our contribution is complementary to retrospective decomposition: we ask when a predictive learner should retain two directional functions rather than collapse their net effect.

\section{PROBLEM SETUP}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $Y_t\in[0,1]$ be a bounded state, let $X_t\in\cX$ denote observed exogenous drivers, and write $Z_t=Y_{t+1}-Y_t$.  All conditional statements below are understood for $P_X$-almost every $x$.

\begin{assumption}[Conditional-mean two-flow model]
\label{ass:mean}
There exist measurable functions $D_0,R_0:\cX\to[0,\infty)$ such that
\begin{align}
 m_0(x,y)
 &:=\E[Z_t\mid X_t=x,Y_t=y]\nonumber\\
 &=D_0(x)(1-y)-R_0(x)y.
\label{eq:drift}
\end{align}
The residual $\varepsilon_t=Z_t-m_0(X_t,Y_t)$ satisfies
$\E[\varepsilon_t\mid X_t,Y_t]=0$ and has finite conditional variance.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:mean} is a statement about a conditional mean, not a causal decomposition.  Causal interpretations of $D_0$ or $R_0$ would require additional assumptions about omitted infrastructure, repair allocation, reporting, and weather measurement.

At a fixed $x$, the two-flow class is
\begin{equation}
\cF_2(x)=\{d(1-y)-ry:d,r\ge 0\}.
\label{eq:f2}
\end{equation}
The parameter-matched one-flow comparator is generated by one signed coefficient $s$,
\begin{equation}
 g_s(y)=\pos{s}(1-y)-\pos{-s}y,
\qquad s\in\Rr,
\label{eq:oneflow}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\cF_1(x)=\{a(1-y):a\ge0\}\cup\{-by:b\ge0\}.
\label{eq:f1}
\end{equation}
A one-flow function may select damage at some driver values and restoration at others.  At a fixed $x$, however, it cannot maintain positive damage and positive restoration simultaneously.

For the conditional law of $Y\mid X=x$, define
\begin{align}
 A(x)&=\E[(1-Y)^2\mid X=x], &
 B(x)&=\E[Y^2\mid X=x],\nonumber\\
 C(x)&=\E[Y(1-Y)\mid X=x], &
 v(x)&=\Var(Y\mid X=x).
\label{eq:moments}
\end{align}
When no ambiguity is possible, we suppress the argument $x$.

\begin{lemma}[Moment identity]
\label{lem:moment}
For $Y\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
 AB-C^2=v.
\label{eq:momentidentity}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu=\E[Y\mid X=x]$ and $q=\E[Y^2\mid X=x]$.  Then
$A=1-2\mu+q$, $B=q$, and $C=\mu-q$.  Direct expansion gives
$AB-C^2=q-\mu^2=v$.
\end{proof}

The implemented state map preserves the unit interval without relying on clipping whenever $D+R\le1$.

\begin{lemma}[State preservation]
\label{lem:state}
For $y\in[0,1]$ and $d,r\ge0$ with $d+r\le1$,
\begin{equation}
 T_{d,r}(y)=y+d(1-y)-ry=d+(1-d-r)y
\end{equation}
satisfies $T_{d,r}(y)\in[d,1-r]\subseteq[0,1]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map is affine and nondecreasing on $[0,1]$; its endpoint values are
$T_{d,r}(0)=d$ and $T_{d,r}(1)=1-r$.
\end{proof}

\section{WHEN IS ONE FLOW ENOUGH?}
\label{sec:oracle-gap}

We first remove estimation error and compare the two function classes at the population level.

\begin{theorem}[Exact oracle one-flow gap]
\label{thm:gap}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:mean}, define
\begin{equation}
 G(x)=\inf_{g\in\cF_1(x)}
 \E[(m_0(x,Y)-g(Y))^2\mid X=x].
\label{eq:Gdef}
\end{equation}
If $v(x)=0$, then $G(x)=0$.  If $v(x)>0$, then both $A(x)$ and $B(x)$ are positive, and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 G(x)=v(x)\min\!\left\{
 \frac{R_0(x)^2}{A(x)},
 \frac{D_0(x)^2}{B(x)}
 \right\}.}
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 G(x)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v(x)>0,\;D_0(x)>0,\;R_0(x)>0.}
\label{eq:gap-strict}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x$ and abbreviate $D_0(x),R_0(x)$ by $D,R$.

If $v=0$, then $Y=\mu$ almost surely.  The scalar drift
$m_0(x,\mu)$ is matched by the damage ray when it is nonnegative and by the restoration ray when it is nonpositive.  The boundary cases $\mu=0$ and $\mu=1$ are matched by $s=D$ and $s=-R$, respectively.  Hence $G=0$.

Suppose $v>0$.  Then $A,B>0$.  On the damage ray, the risk is
\begin{equation}
 L_D(a)=A(D-a)^2-2RC(D-a)+R^2B,
 \qquad a\ge0.
\end{equation}
Its unconstrained minimizer is
\begin{equation}
 a^*=D-R\frac{C}{A},
\label{eq:astar}
\end{equation}
and, when $a^*\ge0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:moment} gives
\begin{equation}
 L_D(a^*)=R^2\left(B-\frac{C^2}{A}\right)
          =R^2\frac{v}{A}.
\label{eq:damage-gap}
\end{equation}
Similarly, the restoration-ray minimizer and its interior risk are
\begin{equation}
 b^*=R-D\frac{C}{B},
 \qquad
 L_R(b^*)=D^2\frac{v}{B}.
\label{eq:bstar}
\end{equation}
The two unconstrained coefficients cannot both be negative.  Indeed,
$a^*<0$ and $b^*<0$ would imply $DA<RC$ and $RB<DC$; multiplying these
strict inequalities contradicts $AB>C^2$, which follows from $v>0$ (the
cases $D=0$ or $R=0$ are immediate).  Moreover, if $a^*<0$, then $R>0$,
$C>0$, and $D/R<C/A<\sqrt{B/A}$, so $D^2/B<R^2/A$.  Hence the restoration
branch is feasible and has the smaller displayed risk.  The symmetric
statement holds if $b^*<0$.  Therefore the best feasible ray has risk equal
to the minimum in \eqref{eq:gap}.  Strictness in \eqref{eq:gap-strict}
follows because $A,B>0$ when $v>0$.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives both the necessity of two flows and the best direction when one flow is imposed.

\begin{corollary}[Oracle branch]
\label{cor:branch}
When $v(x)>0$, the damage-only branch is oracle whenever
\begin{equation}
 \frac{R_0(x)^2}{A(x)}
 \le
 \frac{D_0(x)^2}{B(x)},
\end{equation}
and the restoration-only branch is oracle otherwise.  The corresponding coefficients are \eqref{eq:astar} and \eqref{eq:bstar}, with the feasible branch selected as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Integrated population gap]
\label{cor:global}
Let $\cG_1$ contain all measurable predictors
$g_s(x,y)=\pos{s(x)}(1-y)-\pos{-s(x)}y$.  Under the integrability conditions in Assumption~\ref{ass:mean},
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{g_s\in\cG_1}
 \E[(m_0(X,Y)-g_s(X,Y))^2]
 =\E[G(X)].
\label{eq:globalgap}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Conditional expectation gives a lower bound of $\E[G(X)]$ for every measurable $s$.  The explicit branch rule in Corollary~\ref{cor:branch}, together with the point-mass construction in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, supplies a measurable pointwise minimizer, attaining the bound.
\end{proof}

For fixed conditional mean $\mu\in(0,1)$ and fixed positive $D,R$, the gap increases with state dispersion: $A=(1-\mu)^2+v$ and $B=\mu^2+v$, and both $vR^2/A$ and $vD^2/B$ are strictly increasing on the feasible range of $v$.  Thus conditional state variation does two jobs: it exposes the misspecification of one flow, and, as shown next, it makes the two flows identifiable.

\section{CAN THE TWO FLOWS BE LEARNED?}
\label{sec:identification}

Define
\begin{equation}
 \phi(y)=\begin{bmatrix}1-y\\-y\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 Q(x)=\E[\phi(Y)\phi(Y)^\top\mid X=x].
\label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{align}
 m_0(x,y)&=\phi(y)^\top\beta_0(x),\nonumber\\
 \beta_0(x)&=(D_0(x),R_0(x))^\top.
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Identification geometry]
\label{thm:identification}
For $Y\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 \det Q(x)=v(x),
 \qquad
 v(x)\le\lambda_{\min}(Q(x))\le2v(x),}
\label{eq:eigenbounds}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \frac12\le\lambda_{\max}(Q(x))\le1.
\label{eq:lmax}
\end{equation}
Consequently, $D_0(x)$ and $R_0(x)$ are identified from the conditional transition mean on the support of $Y\mid X=x$ if and only if $v(x)>0$.  If $v(x)=0$ and $Y=\mu(x)$ almost surely, only
\begin{equation}
 (1-\mu(x))D_0(x)-\mu(x)R_0(x)
\end{equation}
is identified.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The matrix $Q$ is
\begin{equation}
 Q=\begin{bmatrix}A&-C\\-C&B\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
so Lemma~\ref{lem:moment} gives $\det Q=v$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \|\phi(Y)\|_2^2=(1-Y)^2+Y^2\le1,
\end{equation}
which implies $\lambda_{\max}(Q)\le1$.  For
$q=(1,-1)^\top/\sqrt2$, we have
$q^\top\phi(Y)=1/\sqrt2$ for every $Y$, and hence
$q^\top Qq=1/2$.  Thus $\lambda_{\max}(Q)\ge1/2$.  For a positive-definite
$2\times2$ matrix, $\lambda_{\min}=\det(Q)/\lambda_{\max}$, giving
\eqref{eq:eigenbounds}; the singular case follows by continuity.

If $v>0$, $Q$ is nonsingular and equality of two conditional mean functions implies equality of their coefficient vectors.  If $v=0$, the conditional support contains one state and only the displayed linear combination enters the mean.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Continuous drivers and estimation]
Theorem~\ref{thm:identification} is a population statement about a regular
conditional distribution at $x$; it does not require duplicate observations
with exactly equal continuous covariates.  Estimating $D_0(x)$ and $R_0(x)$
from finite data does require information sharing across nearby driver values,
through smoothness, a kernel, a sieve, or a neural parameterization.  The theorem
therefore identifies the local rank condition but does not by itself provide a
nonparametric convergence rate.
\end{remark}

The result concerns identification of the functions on the conditional support, not identification of neural-network weights.  It also explains why state prediction can be accurate while rate recovery is poor.  If $\beta$ is any candidate coefficient function, then
\begin{align}
 &\E\!\left[(\phi(Y)^\top\{\beta(X)-\beta_0(X)\})^2\right]\nonumber\\
 &\quad=\E\!\left[(\beta(X)-\beta_0(X))^\top Q(X)
       (\beta(X)-\beta_0(X))\right].
\label{eq:excessrisk}
\end{align}
Errors in the weak eigen-direction receive weight of order $v(X)$.

\subsection{Finite-sample information}

At a fixed $x$, let $\Phi\in\Rr^{n\times2}$ have rows
$\phi(y_i)^\top$.  Under homoskedastic Gaussian noise with variance $\sigma^2$,
$\mathcal I=\sigma^{-2}\Phi^\top\Phi$ is the Fisher information for $(D,R)$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-sample information]
\label{prop:information}
With empirical variance defined using denominator $n$,
\begin{align}
 \det(\Phi^\top\Phi)
 &=\sum_{i<j}(y_i-y_j)^2
 =n^2\widehat{\Var}(y),
\label{eq:detfinite}\\
 \det(\mathcal I)
 &=\frac{n^2\widehat{\Var}(y)}{\sigma^4}.
\label{eq:fisherdet}
\end{align}
If $\Phi$ has rank two, ordinary least squares satisfies
\begin{align}
 \Var(\widehat D\mid\Phi)
 &=\frac{\sigma^2\sum_i y_i^2}
        {\det(\Phi^\top\Phi)},\nonumber\\
 \Var(\widehat R\mid\Phi)
 &=\frac{\sigma^2\sum_i(1-y_i)^2}
        {\det(\Phi^\top\Phi)}.
\label{eq:olsvar}
\end{align}
Thus
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Var(\widehat R\mid\Phi)}
      {\Var(\widehat D\mid\Phi)}
 =\frac{\sum_i(1-y_i)^2}{\sum_i y_i^2}.
\label{eq:varianceratio}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:varianceratio} is a \emph{variance ratio}, not a precision ratio.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Binet gives
\begin{equation}
 \det(\Phi^\top\Phi)
 =\sum_{i<j}\det\!\begin{bmatrix}
 \phi(y_i)^\top\\ \phi(y_j)^\top
 \end{bmatrix}^{\!2}.
\end{equation}
The two-row determinant is $y_i-y_j$, and the pairwise-difference identity yields \eqref{eq:detfinite}.  The remaining statements follow from
\begin{equation}
 \Cov(\widehat\beta\mid\Phi)
 =\sigma^2(\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\section{WHEN IS THE SECOND FLOW WORTH ESTIMATING?}
\label{sec:finite-sample}

Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} is a population statement.  A fitted two-flow model pays for an additional degree of freedom.  We make that cost explicit in a local benchmark while carefully separating it from the constrained neural estimator used in experiments.

Fix states $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ and let
\begin{equation}
 Z=m+\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon\sim\cN(0,\sigma^2I_n),
\label{eq:fixeddesign}
\end{equation}
where $m_i=D(1-y_i)-Ry_i$.  Let $\cS_2$ be the two-dimensional span of the columns $(1-y_i)_{i=1}^n$ and $(-y_i)_{i=1}^n$.  Let $\cS_D$ and $\cS_R$ be the corresponding one-dimensional spans.  An oracle chooses
\begin{equation}
 \cS_1^*\in\argmin_{\cS\in\{\cS_D,\cS_R\}}
 \|(I-P_{\cS})m\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $P_{\cS}$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\cS$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 G_n=\frac1n\|(I-P_1)m\|_2^2,
 \qquad P_1=P_{\cS_1^*},\quad P_2=P_{\cS_2}.
\label{eq:Gn}
\end{equation}

The estimators below are \emph{span-relaxed}: after the oracle chooses a branch, the estimated scalar coefficient is not truncated to be nonnegative.  This relaxation gives an exact degree-of-freedom benchmark; it is not the exact risk of \texttt{net\_scaled} or of the bounded neural two-flow model.

\begin{theorem}[Oracle-span bias--variance benchmark]
\label{thm:threshold}
Assume $\rank(\Phi)=2$ and define
$\widehat m_1=P_1Z$ and $\widehat m_2=P_2Z$.  Then
\begin{align}
 \E\!\left[\frac1n\|\widehat m_2-m\|_2^2\right]
 &=\frac{2\sigma^2}{n},
\label{eq:risk2}\\
 \E\!\left[\frac1n\|\widehat m_1-m\|_2^2\right]
 &=G_n+\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
\label{eq:risk1}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 \frac1n\left(\|\widehat m_1-m\|_2^2
             -\|\widehat m_2-m\|_2^2\right)
 \;\overset{d}{=}\;
 G_n-\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\chi_1^2.}
\label{eq:chisqdiff}
\end{equation}
Write $\mathrm{Err}_k=n^{-1}\|\widehat m_k-m\|_2^2$ for $k\in\{1,2\}$.
Consequently, with
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_n=\frac{nG_n}{\sigma^2},
\label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
we have
\begin{align}
 \E[\mathrm{Err}_1-\mathrm{Err}_2]>0
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Gamma_n>1,
\label{eq:meanthreshold}\\
 \Pp(\mathrm{Err}_2<\mathrm{Err}_1)
 &=F_{\chi_1^2}(\Gamma_n).
\label{eq:probthreshold}
\end{align}
If an independent response $\widetilde Z=m+\widetilde\varepsilon$ is observed at the same design points, then
$\E[n^{-1}\|\widetilde Z-\widehat m_k\|_2^2]
=\sigma^2+\E[\mathrm{Err}_k]$ for both $k$.  Hence the same expected-risk
threshold holds for prediction of a fresh response at the fixed design.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $m\in\cS_2$,
$\widehat m_2-m=P_2\varepsilon$, whose expected squared norm is
$\sigma^2\tr(P_2)=2\sigma^2$.  Also,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat m_1-m=-(I-P_1)m+P_1\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
The two terms are orthogonal, so the expected squared norm is
$nG_n+\sigma^2\tr(P_1)=nG_n+\sigma^2$.
Because $\cS_1^*\subset\cS_2$, the matrix $P_\Delta=P_2-P_1$ is an orthogonal rank-one projector.  Therefore
\begin{align}
 \|\widehat m_1-m\|_2^2-\|\widehat m_2-m\|_2^2
 &=nG_n-\|P_\Delta\varepsilon\|_2^2,
\end{align}
and $\|P_\Delta\varepsilon\|_2^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi_1^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the threshold does and does not imply]
\label{rem:threshold}
Equation~\eqref{eq:meanthreshold} exactly isolates one additional linear degree of freedom under a fixed design and oracle branch selection.  The actual one-flow class is a union of nonnegative rays, and the implemented models are nonlinear, regularized, and shared across nearby driver values.  Hence $\Gamma_n=1$ is a principled local reference, not an exact universal decision boundary for neural-network generalization.  The empirical question is whether the population gap in Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} exceeds the \emph{effective} additional estimation and misspecification cost of the second neural function.
\end{remark}

For completeness, Appendix~\ref{app:nnls} gives the exact scalar risk of the nonnegative least-squares estimator on a fixed ray and shows explicitly how a boundary can shift the variance penalty away from $\sigma^2/n$.

\section{EVENT SHIFT AS AN EXTENSION}
\label{sec:eventshift}

The one-flow oracle depends on the conditional state distribution.  This does not define the paper's central question, but it explains why a collapsed flow can be fragile under event transfer.

Let $P$ and $Q$ denote source and target environments.

\begin{assumption}[Structural invariance for the transfer calculation]
\label{ass:invariance}
At the fixed driver value $x$, the conditional mean functions $D_0(x)$ and
$R_0(x)$ are common to $P$ and $Q$; the environments may differ in the
conditional distribution of $Y\mid X=x$ and in the conditional noise law.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:invariance} is needed only for the transfer decomposition.
If event-specific omitted variables change the two conditional flows themselves,
the formula below does not isolate pure projection shift.  Add a subscript to the
moments in \eqref{eq:moments}.  Consider the damage branch.  Its unconstrained
source coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 a_P^*=D-R\frac{C_P}{A_P}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Source-to-target projection shift]
\label{prop:shift}
If $a_P^*\ge0$ and $a_Q^*\ge0$, then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 \cR_Q^D(a_P^*)
 =R^2\frac{v_Q}{A_Q}
 +R^2A_Q\left(
 \frac{C_P}{A_P}-\frac{C_Q}{A_Q}
 \right)^2,}
\label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
where
$\cR_Q^D(a)=\E_Q[(m_0(x,Y)-a(1-Y))^2\mid X=x]$.
For arbitrary signs, let $a_e^+=\max(0,a_e^*)$.  The boundary-safe identity is
\begin{equation}
 \cR_Q^D(a_P^+)
 =\cR_Q^D(a_Q^+)
 +A_Q\left\{(a_P^+-a_Q^*)^2-(a_Q^+-a_Q^*)^2\right\},
\label{eq:shiftboundary}
\end{equation}
and the bracketed term is nonnegative.  The restoration branch is symmetric.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quadratic target risk completes the square as
\begin{equation}
 \cR_Q^D(a)=R^2\frac{v_Q}{A_Q}+A_Q(a-a_Q^*)^2.
\end{equation}
Substitution yields \eqref{eq:shift}.  Since $a_Q^+$ is the Euclidean projection of $a_Q^*$ onto $[0,\infty)$, it minimizes $(a-a_Q^*)^2$ over feasible $a$, which gives \eqref{eq:shiftboundary} and nonnegativity.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:shift} adds a transfer penalty to the target oracle gap; it does not assert that event holdout must make a finite-sample two-flow neural network better on every event.

\section{NEURAL TWO-FLOW PREDICTOR}
\label{sec:model}

The proposed predictor preserves the two-flow state equation and uses separate bounded neural functions,
\begin{align}
 D_\theta(x)&=c_D\,\sigma(f_D(x;\theta_D)),\nonumber\\
 R_\theta(x)&=c_R\,\sigma(f_R(x;\theta_R)),
\label{eq:rates}\end{align}
\begin{equation}
 \widehat Y_{t+1}
 =\widehat Y_t+D_\theta(X_t)(1-\widehat Y_t)
                  -R_\theta(X_t)\widehat Y_t.
\label{eq:neuralstate}
\end{equation}
Both $f_D$ and $f_R$ are two-layer ReLU networks of width 32.  The caps satisfy $c_D+c_R\le1$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:state} makes numerical clipping inactive for model-generated trajectories.

The theorem-matched comparator uses one signed width-48 network $s_\eta(x)$,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat Y_{t+1}
 =\widehat Y_t+\pos{s_\eta(X_t)}(1-\widehat Y_t)
                  -\pos{-s_\eta(X_t)}\widehat Y_t.
\label{eq:netscaled}
\end{equation}
The widths are chosen so that the parameter counts differ by less than one percent.  Equations~\eqref{eq:neuralstate} and \eqref{eq:netscaled} share inputs, state scaling, optimizer, masks, initialization rule, and training budget.  Their intended difference is whether both directions can be positive at the same driver value.

For source event $e$, let $L_{\mathrm{roll},e}$ be masked 24-hour rollout mean squared error and let $L_{\mathrm{step},e}$ be masked teacher-forced one-step error.  The fixed training objective is
\begin{equation}
 L(\theta)=\frac1{|\cE_{\mathrm{train}}|}
 \sum_{e\in\cE_{\mathrm{train}}}
 \left\{
 \frac12L_{\mathrm{roll},e}
 +\frac12L_{\mathrm{step},e}
 \right\}.
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
The one-step term aligns estimation with the conditional transition risk used in the theory; it is an optimization choice, not an independent contribution.

\section{DISCUSSION}
\label{sec:discussion}

The theory separates necessity, learnability, and finite-sample usefulness.  The oracle gap $G(x)$ answers whether a one-flow conditional mean is structurally sufficient.  The eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(Q(x))$ answers whether the two coefficients are locally distinguishable.  The effective estimation penalty answers whether the second learned function repays its variance and misspecification cost.  Conflating these questions would incorrectly turn identifiability into a performance theorem.

Several limitations are essential.  First, the exact threshold $\Gamma_n=1$ belongs to the relaxed local span benchmark, not to arbitrary neural networks.  Second, the learned functions are conditional predictive components, not causal damage and crew-productivity parameters.  Third, a current-driver scalar-state model may omit delayed damage, restoration age, infrastructure, and repair-allocation variables.  Fourth, event-held-out evaluation contains few independent storms even when it contains many county trajectories.  Finally, known reanalysis weather favors every weather-driven method equally but does not represent an operational end-to-end weather forecast.

The paper deliberately stops before adding memory, semiparametric structure, event embeddings, or additional damage heads.  Such extensions may improve absolute accuracy, but they would obscure the primary comparison between one net flow and two opposing flows.

\section{CONCLUSION}

A signed net update is not always a harmless compression of two opposing processes.  For bounded-state dynamics, the exact loss of that compression is governed by the simultaneous activity of both flows and by conditional state dispersion.  The same dispersion controls local identifiability, while finite samples impose an opposing complexity cost.  This yields a precise answer at the representation level, an explicit local bias--variance benchmark, and a focused empirical test: use one flow when one process is absent or the data cannot distinguish the processes; estimate two when their oracle gap is large enough to repay the second function.

\section{ADDITIONAL PROOFS AND BOUNDARY CASES}

\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Measurable minimizer for Corollary~\ref{cor:global}}

On $\{v>0\}$, define the two candidate interior risks
$g_D=R_0^2v/A$ and $g_R=D_0^2v/B$.  If $g_D\le g_R$ and
$a^*=D_0-R_0C/A\ge0$, choose $s^*=a^*$.  Otherwise choose
$s^*=-b^*$, where $b^*=R_0-D_0C/B\ge0$ by the feasibility argument in
Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}.  The symmetric case is analogous.  On $\{v=0\}$,
write $Y=\mu(X)$ conditionally and choose
\begin{equation}
 s^*(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 m_0(x,\mu)/(1-\mu), & m_0(x,\mu)\ge0,\;\mu<1,\\
 m_0(x,\mu)/\mu, & m_0(x,\mu)<0,\;\mu>0,\\
 D_0(x), & \mu=0,\\
 -R_0(x), & \mu=1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the second line $m_0/\mu<0$, so $g_{s^*}(\mu)=m_0$.  Every quantity in this construction is measurable under Assumption~\ref{ass:mean}.

\subsection{The weakly identified direction}

Let $\mu=\E[Y\mid X=x]$ and define
\begin{equation}
 n_\mu=\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\1-\mu\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \phi(Y)^\top n_\mu=\mu-Y,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
 n_\mu^\top Qn_\mu=v.
\end{equation}
Perturbing the rates by $c n_\mu$ changes their Euclidean norm by order $c$ but changes one-step prediction energy by exactly $c^2v$.  This gives a concrete form of the weakly identified direction when $v$ is small.

\subsection{Cauchy--Binet determinant identity}

For rows $\phi(y_i)^\top=(1-y_i,-y_i)$,
\begin{equation}
 \det\begin{bmatrix}1-y_i&-y_i\\1-y_j&-y_j\end{bmatrix}
 =y_i-y_j.
\end{equation}
Cauchy--Binet therefore yields
\begin{equation}
 \det(\Phi^\top\Phi)
 =\sum_{i<j}(y_i-y_j)^2
 =n\sum_i y_i^2-\left(\sum_i y_i\right)^2.
\end{equation}
The final expression is $n^2$ times empirical variance with denominator $n$.

\section{NONNEGATIVE-RAY ESTIMATION}
\label{app:nnls}

Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} uses a span-relaxed estimator.  The following
calculation gives the exact risk on one nonnegative ray and shows why an active
boundary changes the one-dimensional complexity penalty.

\begin{proposition}[Exact risk on a fixed nonnegative ray]
\label{prop:nnls}
Let $q\in\Rr^n$ satisfy $\|q\|_2=1$, decompose an arbitrary mean vector as
$m=\alpha q+r$ with $r\perp q$, and observe $Z=m+\varepsilon$ with
$\varepsilon\sim\cN(0,\sigma^2I_n)$.  The least-squares estimator over the ray
$\{\theta q:\theta\ge0\}$ is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat m_+=q\pos{\alpha+\sigma W},
 \qquad W\sim\cN(0,1).
\end{equation}
Writing $t=\alpha/\sigma$, its mean-squared fitted-mean error is
\begin{equation}
 \E\|\widehat m_+-m\|_2^2
 =\|r\|_2^2+\sigma^2\rho(t),
\label{eq:rho-risk}
\end{equation}
where, for every $t\in\Rr$,
\begin{equation}
 \rho(t)=\Phi(t)-t\varphi(t)+t^2\Phi(-t).
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
The deterministic approximation error of the ray is
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{\theta\ge0}\|\theta q-m\|_2^2
 =\|r\|_2^2+\alpha_-^2,
 \qquad \alpha_-:=\pos{-\alpha}.
\label{eq:ray-gap}
\end{equation}
Hence the estimation excess above the constrained oracle is
$\sigma^2\{\rho(t)-t_-^2\}$, where $t_-=\pos{-t}$.  In particular,
$\rho(0)=1/2$ and $\rho(t)\to1$ as $t\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The orthogonal decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
 \|\widehat m_+-m\|_2^2
 =\|r\|_2^2+
   \{\pos{\alpha+\sigma W}-\alpha\}^2.
\end{equation}
When $W\ge-t$, the scalar error is $\sigma W$; otherwise it is $-\alpha$.
Therefore
\begin{align}
 &\sigma^{-2}\E[\{\pos{\alpha+\sigma W}-\alpha\}^2]\nonumber\\
 &\quad=\E[W^2\one\{W\ge-t\}]+t^2\Phi(-t)\\
 &\quad=\Phi(t)-t\varphi(t)+t^2\Phi(-t).
\end{align}
Projection of $\alpha$ onto $[0,\infty)$ gives \eqref{eq:ray-gap}.  The stated
boundary values follow directly.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:nnls} shows that the literal one-dimensional variance
penalty can be smaller than $\sigma^2$ near an active boundary and depends on
the branch signal-to-noise ratio.  This reinforces the interpretation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold} as a transparent span benchmark rather than an exact
decision rule for the constrained neural models.

\section{EVENT-SHIFT MINIMAX COROLLARY}
\label{app:minimax}

For two environments $P,Q$ whose damage-branch optima are interior,
\begin{equation}
 \cR_e^D(a)-\cR_e^D(a_e^*)=A_e(a-a_e^*)^2.
\end{equation}
Balancing the two weighted squared distances gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 &\inf_a\max_{e\in\{P,Q\}}
 \{\cR_e^D(a)-\cR_e^D(a_e^*)\}\\
 &\quad=
 \frac{A_PA_Q}{(\sqrt{A_P}+\sqrt{A_Q})^2}
 R^2\left(\frac{C_P}{A_P}-\frac{C_Q}{A_Q}\right)^2.
\end{split}
\label{eq:minimax}
\end{equation}
The quantity is strictly positive whenever $R>0$ and the projection ratios differ.  The restoration expression follows by replacing $(A,R,C/A)$ with $(B,D,C/B)$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:minimax} is a robustness statement about the collapsed representation, not a finite-sample dominance theorem.
